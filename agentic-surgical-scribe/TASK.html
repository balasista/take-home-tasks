1 · Scenario

A hospital surgical services group runs a high-volume laparoscopic cholecystectomy service. The operative notes are the problem, not the surgery.

Surgeons dictate the operative note after the case, often hours later and sometimes the next morning. By then the detail has faded. Three things go wrong, repeatedly:

  • The note is late. Billing cannot code the case until the note is signed, so revenue sits in a queue.
  • The note is thin. Required documentation elements go missing. The one the quality committee cares about most is the Critical View of Safety: if the surgeon achieved it and did not say so in the note, the case reads as unsafe in the audit even though it was not.
  • The coding is wrong or defensive. A cholangiogram that was discussed but never performed gets coded as performed. A case converted to open gets coded as laparoscopic. Both are the kind of error that turns into a payer clawback.

The group already records ambient audio in the operating room and runs it through a commodity speech-to-text service. They now have transcripts and no idea what to do with them. They have asked CODVO.AI to build the layer that turns a transcript into a signed note, and they have asked for an architect who can defend the design to their CMIO and their compliance officer, not only to their engineers.

You are that architect.

2 · Your objective

Build a reproducible system that takes one operating-room transcript and produces four things:

A
A structured operative record
Every clinical field the institution requires, each one carrying the transcript utterances that support it and a confidence.
Scored
B
A draft narrative operative note
In the institution's template and register, ready for a surgeon to read, edit and sign.
Scored
C
A coding proposal
CPT and ICD-10 selected from the supplied code universe, each with a written rationale and its evidence.
Scored
D
A review queue for the surgeon
What the system is unsure about, what conflicts, and what the transcript never said. Ranked, so a busy surgeon reads the top three.
Scored
What we are really assessing

This is an architect role. We care far more about how you decompose the problem, how you keep a generative system honest, how you measure it, and what you traded away, than about the polish of any single note. A modest system whose failure modes you can name and demonstrate beats an impressive one you cannot account for.

3 · The data package

Everything is under data/. Read docs/data_dictionary.md and docs/documentation_standard.md before you write a prompt.

PathWhat it is
data/transcripts/*.jsonl 20 cases. One utterance per line, in time order. Roughly 300 to 700 utterances per case, which is 6k to 10k tokens of transcript.
data/gold/*.json Structured operative records for 12 of the 20 cases, produced by a clinical reviewer. This is your labeled set. The remaining 8 cases have no gold record.
data/gold/*.md The signed operative note for those same 12 cases. This is your style corpus and your narrative reference.
data/reference/cpt_codes.csv The allowed CPT universe with official descriptors. Codes outside this file are not valid submissions.
data/reference/icd10_codes.csv The allowed ICD-10-CM universe with descriptors.
docs/documentation_standard.md The institution's rules: which note sections are mandatory, which fields must be numeric, what has to be stated explicitly rather than implied.
schemas/op_record.schema.json The output contract as JSON Schema. Your submission is validated against this file before it is scored.
data/sample_case/ One case end to end: transcript, gold record, signed note, and the expected output files. Build against this first.

Transcript format

One JSON object per line. Fields are documented in full in the data dictionary.

{"case_id": "CASE_0007", "utt_id": "u0142", "t_start_s": 1284.6, "t_end_s": 1291.2,
 "speaker": "SPK_2", "speaker_role_hint": "surgeon", "asr_confidence": 0.83,
 "text": "okay I'm happy with that, critical view achieved, two structures only"}

speaker_role_hint is what the diarization service guessed. It is a hint. It is not always right, and there are more people in the room than there are roles in the schema.

The case mix is not uniform, and we are not telling you what is in it

Most of these are routine cases. A minority are not. A pipeline tuned only to the straightforward case will be found out on the live set, and the interesting part of the presentation will be why.

4 · Why this is hard

These are transcripts of a working operating room, not dictations. The difficulty is not the length. It is that speech is a bad source of truth and you are being asked to produce a legal medical document from it.

Some of what you will meet, stated generally, because working out the specifics is the job:

  • People correct themselves. A number said once is not a number. Later statements can supersede earlier ones, and sometimes the earlier one was right.
  • People discuss things they then do not do. Considering a cholangiogram, deciding against it, and moving on is a very common exchange. It reads almost identically to performing one.
  • Negation and hedging carry clinical weight. "That might be the cystic artery" during dissection is not a finding. "No evidence of bile duct injury" is a finding, and it is the opposite of the one a careless extractor will record.
  • Not everyone in the room is dictating. Anesthesia, the circulator and the scrub tech all speak. So does the radio. Attribution matters.
  • The speech-to-text is imperfect. Anatomical terms, instrument brand names and drug names are where it breaks, and it breaks confidently.
  • The template asks for things nobody said. Some required fields have no support anywhere in the transcript. What your system does at that moment is the single most important behavior in this assignment.
The rule that shapes everything else

A confident wrong value is worse than an honest null. We score abstention as correct when the transcript genuinely does not contain the fact. Read that again before you design your prompts: the system is rewarded for saying "not documented" and punished for filling the gap plausibly.

5 · The output contract

This part is fixed. Everything upstream of it is your decision.

For each case your system writes one file, out/<case_id>.json, validating against schemas/op_record.schema.json. Abbreviated shape:

{
  "case_id": "CASE_T03",
  "fields": {
    "procedure_performed":    {"value": "Laparoscopic cholecystectomy",
                               "evidence": ["u0031","u0044"], "confidence": 0.94},
    "converted_to_open":      {"value": false, "evidence": ["u0402"], "confidence": 0.88},
    "cholangiogram_performed":{"value": false, "evidence": ["u0217"], "confidence": 0.71},
    "critical_view_of_safety":{"value": "achieved_and_stated",
                               "evidence": ["u0142"], "confidence": 0.90},
    "estimated_blood_loss_ml":{"value": 25,   "evidence": ["u0511"], "confidence": 0.95},
    "drain_placed":           {"value": null, "evidence": [],        "confidence": 0.55},
    "specimen_disposition":   {"value": "sent to pathology",
                               "evidence": ["u0530"], "confidence": 0.86},
    "complications":          {"value": [],   "evidence": ["u0544"], "confidence": 0.80},
    "counts_correct":         {"value": true, "evidence": ["u0561"], "confidence": 0.92}
  },
  "coding": {
    "cpt":   [{"code":"47562","rationale":"...","evidence":["u0031","u0402"],"confidence":0.9}],
    "icd10": [{"code":"K80.20","rationale":"...","evidence":["u0012"],"confidence":0.85}]
  },
  "review_queue": [
    {"rank":1, "field":"estimated_blood_loss_ml", "reason":"two conflicting statements",
     "evidence":["u0498","u0511"]}
  ],
  "note_markdown": "PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS: ...",
  "run": {"models":["..."], "prompt_version":"...", "input_tokens":0, "output_tokens":0,
          "usd":0.0, "wall_clock_s":0.0, "llm_calls":0}
}

Three rules about this object:

  • Every field carries its evidence. evidence is a list of utt_id values from that case's transcript. We check that the cited utterances actually support the value. Citing the whole transcript is not evidence.
  • null is a legitimate value and means "the transcript does not say." It is not a failure state and it is not an error to be logged.
  • run is not optional. If you cannot tell us what a note cost and how long it took, you have not built a production system.

Command-line contract

One command, no interactive prompts, reading a directory of transcripts and writing a directory of records:

python -m scribe.run --in data/transcripts/ --out out/ [--mock]

You may extend the schema with extra fields. You may not remove or rename what is there.

6 · Choose your architecture

We are handing you a contract and a corpus, not a design. How you get from transcript to record is yours to choose and yours to defend.

Option A

Single-pass extraction

One capable model, one schema-constrained call, the whole transcript in context. Cheapest to build, easiest to reason about, most deterministic. If this is enough, say so and show it.

Option B

Fixed pipeline

A deterministic chain of specialized calls: segment, extract, code, compose, verify. No autonomy, full control over the path, each stage testable on its own.

Option C

Supervised multi-agent

A supervisor routing to specialist agents with tools, dynamic control flow, and a critic that can send work back. Most capable, hardest to keep deterministic, most expensive per note.

Option D

Your own

Something else, if you can justify it against the same yardsticks: accuracy, cost, latency, determinism and how a failure gets diagnosed at 2am.

Expect this question

Why did you not choose the simplest thing that works? The transcripts fit in a single context window. That is deliberate. It means nothing is forcing you to decompose, so if you decompose it has to be because decomposition buys you something you can name and preferably measure. "It is agentic" is not a reason. An architect who ships Option A with evidence that B and C did not earn their cost is a stronger candidate than one who ships C because C is the fashionable answer.

7 · Mandatory capabilities

Whatever architecture you pick, these six have to be present and you have to be able to point at where they live in your code.

1
Codes come from a tool, not from memory
CPT and ICD-10 selection must go through a lookup against data/reference/. A model reciting a code from its weights is a clawback waiting to happen, and it is the one behavior a compliance officer will test you on.
Required
2
Evidence binding
Every emitted value traces to specific utterances, or it is null. The narrative note must be derivable from the structured record, not written freehand alongside it.
Required
3
An independent verification step
Something that re-reads the draft against the transcript and can reject or revise it. The generator marking its own homework does not count; show us why your check is independent of the thing it checks.
Required
4
Guardrails inside the pipeline
Identifiers spoken aloud in the room must be handled before anything leaves your process boundary. Out-of-scope requests and prompt-injection attempts from transcript text must not steer the run. This is control-flow enforcement, not an HTTP middleware.
Required
5
Calibrated confidence and escalation
Confidence has to mean something: low-confidence fields should be wrong more often than high-confidence ones, and you should be able to show that on your labeled set. The review queue is how a surgeon consumes it.
Required
6
A degraded mode that still runs
--mock runs the whole pipeline offline against recorded fixtures, with no network. It exists so a rate limit cannot kill your demo, and so we can see the orchestration independently of the model.
Required

8 · Build your own evaluation

We are not giving you a metric. Deciding what "good" means for a generative system that writes medical documents is a substantial part of this assignment.

You have 12 gold records. Build a harness that runs your system over them and reports numbers. At minimum we expect you to have an opinion about:

DimensionThe question it answers
Field accuracy How often is a populated field right? Handle the field types differently: a numeric blood loss is not scored like a free-text disposition.
Hallucination rate How often does the system assert something the transcript does not support? This is the number the CMIO will ask for first.
Abstention quality When it says null, is it right to? Both failure directions matter: inventing a value, and refusing to state one that was said clearly.
Evidence precision Do the cited utterances actually support the value, or is the citation decorative?
Coding accuracy Exact code match, and separately, whether the rationale holds up.
Calibration Does confidence track correctness? A system that is 0.95 confident about everything has no confidence signal at all.
Note quality Would a surgeon sign it without rewriting it? Say how you measured this rather than asserting it.
Cost and latency Dollars and seconds per note, at p50 and p95. This service runs a few hundred cases a month.
Determinism Run the same case twice. How much does the output move, and does that matter for a document that gets signed?
Report the numbers you got, not the numbers you wanted

A submission that says "hallucination rate 6.2%, concentrated in specimen disposition and drain placement, and here is why" is stronger than one claiming 0%. We will look for the failure cases ourselves, and it goes better when you found them first.

9 · Stretch, if the core is solid

Only after the core works end to end, and only one of these. We would rather see one done properly than four sketched.

  • Cross-case questioning. A conversational layer over the corpus of generated records: "how many cases this month had no Critical View of Safety documented, and which surgeons?" Show how you keep it from answering questions the data cannot support.
  • Streaming mode. Produce the note progressively as the case runs, aware of surgical phase, so the draft is ready when the patient leaves the room. Explain what breaks when you can no longer see the whole transcript at once.
  • Surgeon style adaptation. Match the individual surgeon's register and structure using the signed notes as a corpus, without letting style leak into clinical content.
  • Cost engineering. Get the per-note cost down by a large factor with model routing or a small model plus escalation, and show on your eval what it cost you in accuracy.
  • Eval as a gate. Wire the harness into CI so a prompt change that regresses hallucination rate fails the build. Show it failing.

10 · The live test

In the final 20 minutes we hand you transcripts you have never seen and run your pipeline on the call.

Four cases, identical schema to data/transcripts/, drawn from the same service. You run one command. We take the out/ directory and score it against records our clinical reviewer prepared.

we check, in this order 1. schema valid does every file validate against op_record.schema.json 2. evidence resolves does every utt_id exist in that case's transcript 3. field accuracy value match against the reviewer's record 4. abstention null where the transcript is genuinely silent 5. hallucination values asserted with no supporting utterance 6. coding CPT and ICD-10 against the reviewer's codes 7. review queue did the top-ranked item point at a real problem 8. run manifest cost and latency actually reported

At least one of the four is not a routine case. Your review queue matters more on that one than your accuracy does.

If it does not run, we cannot score it

Rehearse the exact command end to end the day before, on a clean clone, with a fresh virtual environment. Have --mock tested and ready: if your provider rate-limits you live, you switch to mock and keep talking, and we will score the orchestration. Watching someone debug an import error for twelve minutes tells us something, and it is not what you want it to tell us.

12 · How we score you

Judgment and engineering discipline are weighted above accuracy. The live numbers are one input among several.

DimensionWhat a strong submission shows
Problem framing You understood that the deliverable is a signed legal document, and designed backward from that rather than forward from the transcript.
Architectural restraint Every component earns its place. You can say what each LLM call is for, what it costs, and what happens if you delete it.
Grounding and honesty Evidence binding is real, not decorative. Abstention works. You can demonstrate the system refusing to invent something.
Guardrails Identifier handling and injection resistance are enforced in the control flow and you can show them firing, not just described in the README.
Evaluation rigor Metrics you chose and justified, an honest read of where the system breaks, and no leakage between the cases you tuned on and the cases you report on.
Production thinking Cost per note, latency, determinism, prompt versioning, what you log, how you would debug a bad note two weeks after it was signed.
Clinical seriousness You engaged with the documentation standard and the coding rules rather than treating them as string-matching targets.
Reproducibility Fresh clone runs. Dependencies pinned, secrets in environment variables, --mock works with the network off.
Communication You defend every decision and name the limitations before we do.

13 · Ground rules

  • AI coding assistants are allowed and expected. You must understand and defend every line. We probe deeply, and the gap between authored and generated code is visible from the outside.
  • Bring your own model and key. Any provider. Keys go in environment variables and never in the repository. We will check.
  • Any framework, or none. LangGraph, LangChain, CrewAI, DSPy, the provider SDK on its own, all fine. If you use a framework, expect to be asked what it bought you that plain code would not have.
  • Budget your spend and report it. Keep the whole build under roughly 25 US dollars of tokens and tell us what it actually came to. An architect who does not know the unit economics of their own system is a risk.
  • Treat the data as if it were real PHI. It is synthetic. Handle it as though it were not: no pasting transcripts into consumer chat interfaces, no third-party services you would not put a patient record through. How you behave here is itself a signal.
  • Do not over-build. Three days. A focused system with an honest eval beats a platform.
  • Document assumptions rather than emailing questions. Stating a reasonable assumption clearly is a signal we value. If something genuinely blocks you, do reach out.
  • Commit incrementally. We read the history. It tells us how you work.

15 · FAQ

Do I get the audio?

No. Speech-to-text is a solved commodity and it is not what we are hiring for. You get transcripts with the recognition errors a real service produces.

Which model should I use?

Your call, and a defensible one either way. We will ask why that model, whether you tried a cheaper one, and what the accuracy difference actually was on your eval.

Can I fine-tune?

Yes, if the result runs inside the live window and you can explain what 12 labeled cases can and cannot teach a model. Prompting and retrieval are entirely acceptable answers here.

Twelve gold records is not much. Is that a mistake?

No. It is the realistic condition. Designing an evaluation on a small labeled set, and being honest about the confidence intervals that implies, is part of the assignment.

What if my system produces more than the schema asks for?

Include it. Extra fields are welcome as long as the required ones are present and valid, since that is what the live scoring reads.

How accurate does it have to be?

There is no threshold. We assess the whole approach. "Here is where it works, here is where it does not, and here is why" is worth more than a number with nothing behind it.

Can I ignore the stretch section?

Yes. A solid core with no stretch scores better than a weak core with one bolted on.
